Your computer has been upgraded to Windows 11, version 24H2. Here's what's new for you — a faster, smoother File Explorer, easier ways to share your Wi-Fi, more control over your privacy, and a handful of small touches that make everyday things a little easier.

A more streamlined File Explorer

Managing your files is quicker and clearer.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER — file-contecxual-menu.png — File Explorer right-click menu with labels ]
  • Right-click a file and the menu now shows clear labels — cut, copy, paste, rename, share, delete — instead of icons you had to guess at.
  • Drag files straight onto a folder name in the address bar at the top of the window to move them there instantly.
  • Compress files into 7-Zip or TAR archives, not just ZIP, right from the same right-click menu.
  • Hovering over File Explorer on the taskbar now shows a preview of every tab you have open, not just one window.

A tidier taskbar

Small touches that make the everyday stuff faster.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER — quick-settings.png — Scrollable Quick Settings panel with Live captions ]
  • Quick Settings — the panel you open from the clock and volume icons — now scrolls through all your toggles, so there's no need to edit the list to find one.
  • Drag any app straight from the Start menu onto the taskbar to pin it.
  • Connecting to Wi-Fi and your VPN takes fewer clicks, with a clearer connecting animation so you know it's working.
  • Live Captions can be switched on directly from the system tray.

More visibility into your privacy

This is the change you're most likely to notice in your first few days on 24H2.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER — access-location-prompt.png — Prompt asking to allow an app to access location ]

Windows now lets you know the moment an app tries to use your location, and warns you if an app asks unexpectedly — so you always know who's asking and can say no. This happens even if location is switched off; the notification is just telling you about the request, not turning anything on for the app.

You can see exactly which apps have used your location under Settings > Privacy & security > Location > Recent activity, and a small location icon appears in the taskbar whenever an app is actively using it.

Prefer not to see the pop-ups? Go to Settings > Privacy & security > Location and turn off Notify when apps request location.

Easier Wi-Fi sharing

Getting a guest, or a second device, online is now a scan away.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER — hotspot-qr-code.png — QR code used to connect to a Wi-Fi network ]
  • Share any saved Wi-Fi network as a QR code, so another device can scan and connect without typing a password.
  • If your laptop and access point both support it, Windows can now connect over Wi-Fi 7 for faster, more reliable wireless speeds.

Smarter battery life

Get more out of a charge, on laptops and desktops alike.

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER — energy-saver.png — Settings, System, Power and battery, Energy saver ]
  • Energy saver now works on desktop PCs too, not just laptops. Turn it on from Quick Settings, or have it switch on automatically at a battery level you choose.
  • Supported laptops can automatically fine-tune screen brightness to stretch your battery further, without a visible difference in what you see.

Printing, made simpler

  • Give your printer a name you'll actually recognize, right from Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners.
  • Pause and resume a single print job from the queue, instead of stopping everything else waiting to print.

Built to work for everyone

[ IMAGE PLACEHOLDER — hearing-aids-sound-properties.png — Sound properties for a hearing aid device ]
  • Bluetooth hearing aids can now pair directly with your PC to stream audio, take calls, and adjust presets from Settings > Accessibility > Hearing devices.
  • Voice Clarity automatically reduces echo and background noise on video calls — already on by default, no setup needed.
  • Narrator reads long documents noticeably faster, especially in Microsoft Edge.

One thing to know

WordPad has been retired and is no longer included in Windows. For documents, use Word; for quick plain text, use Notepad.
